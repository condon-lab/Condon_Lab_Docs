# Revisions 

If your paper is rejected, don’t lose hope; there are lots of options to submit to other journals. You will meet with Laura and discuss next steps. 

If you receive comments back on your manuscript, your job is to create a response to the reviewer's comments. Pay attention to the timeline when your revisions are due back and work with Laura on a timeline right away. You will need time to iterate with Laura and any co-authors on your responses. It's also important to contact your co-authors early to understand their timeline for providing comments. You will need to address ALL comments from the reviewers. 

1. The first step is to draft responses and talk to Laura about your general plan for revisions. You don’t have to agree with every reviewer's comment. You can strategically push back on comments you don’t agree with as long as you have good reasoning for this.  

    To draft your response, copy and paste all editor and reviewer comments into a Word doc. Spend no more than 2-3 hours writing an off-the-cuff response to each, using a different color. You don’t need to actually make changes at this point, just outline what you are thinking of changing, questions you have, or things you want to push back on. 

    Meet with Laura and iterate with co-authors as needed to agree on the high-level strategy for how to respond. 

2. Once you have figured out the plan, you will work on making your revision. In this process, you need to (1) track changes in your Word doc and (2) create a response to reviewers. In some cases, you will also create a high-level letter to the editor summarizing the changes you made.

    Here are some general guidelines for how to respond to comments: 

    - Respond to each comment positively and constructively. Comments can be hard to receive, but remember the goal here is to make your manuscript better so try your best to find constructive responses wherever possible.  
    - Make sure to describe the action you took to address the comment in each response.
    - Ask Laura and your lab mates for examples of reviewer responses to get inspiration on how to reply. 

Once you are ready to resubmit, you will need to accept all changes from Laura or co-authors made on either the revised manuscript or the response document. 

You will need to submit two copies of the revised manuscript: a clean version with all changes accepted with comments deleted, and a version with tracked changes. 

To create the tracked changes document, navigate to the “Review” tab in Word and click “Compare”. For the first document, select the original version you submitted, before any revisions. For the second document, select the newest clean version of the revised manuscript. 

Follow the resubmission instructions from the journal by uploading all required documents and sometimes a new cover letter.
